# Fit rubric

Go through these in order. For each one write a plain sentence, not a number.

## 1. Hard blockers (check first – they end the analysis)

- Work authorization or location.
- Employment type that doesn't work for the user.
- Pay clearly below the user's minimum (from the saved profile).
- A required certificate or licence that is missing.
- Something on the user's `avoid` list that is central to the role.

If one is real: verdict is `needs a check first` or `skip`. Stop there.

## 2. Requirements vs CV

Split requirements into:
- **Must** – "required", "must", "N+ years", or listed first.
- **Nice** – "plus", "advantage", "would be great".

For each Must: `match` / `partial` / `not in CV` / `gap`, with quoted evidence (`cv-check.md`).
Don't count the same line twice. Judge the requirement text, not the tags or the title.
Do the arithmetic on years from the CV dates.

## 3. Seniority vs title

Compare the title with the duties, using the family's level signals (`role-families.md`):
- Who decides – the person, or someone above?
- "Lead", "own", "define", "mentor" = senior or higher scope, whatever the title says.
- "Support", "implement from tickets", "under guidance" = junior or middle scope.
- Years asked vs scope asked.

Say which side the user is on: overqualified, matched, or a stretch – and whether the stretch is the good kind.

## 4. Domain and the work itself

- Is this a product company, a client-service firm, or staff augmentation? What is the actual product or service?
- Does the domain use what the user already knows? Is it new to them? That's fine if the core problems are familiar.
- Is the day-to-day work what the title suggests?

## 5. Tools and methods

- Which tools, methods or systems are named, and which are really used? ("Agile" and "Scrum" are not the same thing as the real process.)
- Anything outside the user's profile that is a Must. Is it learnable quickly, or a real gap?

## 6. Money and format

- Pay stated? Gross or net? Currency? Estimated by the job board or stated by the company?
- Contract type, hours, travel, timezone overlap.
- If unstated: write `not stated`, and put it in the questions.

## 7. Verdict

- `apply` – no blockers, Musts mostly matched, growth is real.
- `apply with questions` – the fit is good but something important is unknown.
- `needs a check first` – one blocker is unverified.
- `skip` – a blocker, or the Musts are mostly gaps, or growth is nil and pay is low.
