# Growth signals

A vacancy rarely says anything about growth. So look for indirect signals,
and mark each one: **stated** (in the text) or **guess** (your reading).

## Signs of real growth

- Scope that is slightly above what the user did: architecture decisions, standards, design reviews.
- A new problem class with familiar tools (real-time, performance at scale, design systems, observability).
- Working next to people stronger than the user: an architect, a tech lead who reviews, a platform team.
- A product that lives for years, not a project with an end date.
- Mentoring is mentioned as a duty, not a perk – means seniors exist and are valued.
- Time for tech debt, testing, refactoring mentioned.
- Promotion from within mentioned with facts (names of paths, examples), not just "we promote".
- Client or stakeholder contact (requirements, trade-offs, estimates).

## Signs of a plateau

- Same stack and same kind of tasks as now, with a longer list of buzzwords.
- "Fast-paced", "wear many hats", "we are like a family" without any process described.
- No mention of review, testing, release process.
- Staff augmentation where the client may end the contract any month.
- Small team where the user would be the only person in their specialty, with nobody to learn from.
- Title says Senior but duties are tickets only.

## Signs of going backwards

- Legacy maintenance with no migration plan.
- Tools or methods older than what the user already uses, with no plan to move on.
- Seniority below the user's current level with no path up and no money to compensate.

## How to write the growth part

1. One line: what the user would learn that they don't do now.
2. One line: what would be the same work.
3. One line: what can't be told from the vacancy – these become questions.

Don't say "great growth opportunities" unless you can name the specific thing.
