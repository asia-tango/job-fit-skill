# Role families

At Setup, read the CV and decide which family (or two) fits. Say it and let the user correct it.
This only changes what to look at and what to ask. The rules in SKILL.md stay the same.
If the role fits no family, use "Other" and derive the same four things from the CV and the vacancy.

For each family: what "scope" looks like, which signals separate levels, extra setup questions,
and extra questions for a vacancy.

## Software engineering (frontend, backend, mobile, devops, QA, data engineering)

- **Scope signals:** who decides architecture, size of the system (traffic, data, users), on-call and ownership, mentoring and review, deployment responsibility.
- **Level signals:** "implements tickets" → middle. "Designs and owns components, reviews, mentors" → senior. "Sets standards across teams" → staff.
- **Setup extras:** main stack and years per tool; what scale you've worked at; on-call acceptable?
- **Vacancy extras:** What does on-call look like? How are architecture decisions made? What is the deploy frequency and who owns incidents? How big is the system (traffic, data volume, team)?
- **Watch for:** a stack tag that doesn't match the requirement text; "fullstack" where one side is a token mention; legacy maintenance without a migration plan.

## Data and analytics (analyst, BI, data science, ML)

- **Scope signals:** who defines the question, decision impact of the work, data volume and quality ownership, stakeholder exposure, production vs ad-hoc analysis.
- **Level signals:** "builds reports to spec" → junior/middle. "Frames the question and defends the answer to executives" → senior.
- **Setup extras:** tools (SQL, Python, BI tool); domains; production models vs analysis only.
- **Vacancy extras:** Where does the data come from and who owns its quality? Is the work answering questions or building products? Who consumes the results? Is there a data engineer, or does the analyst build the pipelines?
- **Watch for:** "data scientist" meaning dashboards; "ML engineer" meaning infrastructure; no mention of data access.

## Management (project, product, engineering management, operations)

- **Scope signals:** team size, budget, who they report to, decision rights, hiring and firing, P&L, stakeholders.
- **Level signals:** "coordinates" → junior. "Owns delivery or budget" → senior. "Sets strategy, owns the roadmap or P&L" → head/director.
- **Setup extras:** team sizes and budgets managed; industry; certifications; people management vs delivery.
- **Vacancy extras:** What can the person in this role decide without approval? What is the budget and who signs off? What is the team's size and its current state (new, shrinking, growing)? What does success look like in 6 months? Why is the seat open?
- **Watch for:** responsibility without authority; "head of" with no team; a certification marked required that the CV doesn't have (a real gap, not a keyword fix).

## Design and research (product, UX, graphic, research)

- **Scope signals:** who sets the product direction, research access to real users, design system ownership, handoff process, how impact is measured.
- **Level signals:** "executes designs" → middle. "Defines the problem and the approach" → senior. "Sets design direction across products" → lead.
- **Setup extras:** portfolio link and what it shows; tools; B2B/B2C; research methods used.
- **Vacancy extras:** Do designers have access to users? Who decides what gets built? Is there a design system, and who maintains it? How is design success measured?
- **Watch for:** "portfolio required" when the CV has no link (ask); "designer" meaning production work; design handoff to developers with no feedback loop.

## Other

Derive these four from the CV and the vacancy text:
1. What does a person at the next level do that the CV doesn't show yet?
2. What decides success in the role, and who measures it?
3. What would be a real gap for the user, and what is only a missing word in the CV?
4. What can't the vacancy tell the user that matters for growth and money?
Then ask the user the setup extras that follow from those.
