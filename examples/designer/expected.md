**What would change the verdict** – if the pay for 20 hours a week is below the saved minimum, skip. If the portfolio isn't shareable, the application is weak whatever the CV says.

**Fix in the CV**
1. Add the portfolio link at the top. This is usually the first thing a hiring manager opens.
2. If the activation lift came from an A/B test, say so in the same line. Add only if true.
3. Move "Built and maintained a design system in Figma with 120 components" up; it answers a stated requirement with a number.

**Growth**
- Stated: mentoring juniors, scaling design systems.
- Guess: a part-time contract gives variety but little room for promotion.
- Can't tell: who sets the product direction and whether designers talk to users.

**Red flags** – no pay stated for a part-time contract. "20 hours per week" may become more in practice (ask).

**Money and format** – "Contract, 20 hours per week, remote." Pay not stated.

**Questions to ask**
- What is the pay for 20 hours a week, and is it gross or net? (not stated)
- Is the contract renewed, and for how long? (stability)
- Do designers have direct access to users, and who decides what gets built? (scope)
- Is the hours cap strict? (risk of hidden overtime)

**Talking points for the application**
1. Lead with the system. Backs: "Built and maintained a design system in Figma with 120 components". Answers: "Experience building and scaling design systems".
2. A measured result. Backs: "Redesigned the onboarding flow, increasing activation from 31% to 44%".
3. Research practice. Backs: "Ran 20+ moderated usability sessions". Answers: "Experience with quantitative research and A/B testing" only in part; say what was done, not more.
4. The gap, said plainly: A/B testing and mentoring aren't in the CV.
5. Check before writing: don't claim a portfolio of "shipped B2B products" without links to show them.
